Create a **scientific infographic illustration for an upright A0 conference poster**, designed to occupy approximately one of the two poster columns.

### Exact output size

* **Physical width: exactly 390 mm**
* **Height: flexible; determine naturally from the composition**
* Do not force a predetermined aspect ratio or height.
* If working at 300 DPI, use approximately **4606 px wide**.
* The composition should be **more compact and less vertically elongated** than a conventional six-panel workflow.

### Concept

Illustrate the complete workflow of a **high-throughput X-ray/CT system for digitising museum artefacts**, from museum packing through transport, robotic loading, scanning, reconstruction, and archival storage.

The six stages are:

**1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 6**

Use a **compact, snaking two-column composition** with an immediately obvious reading path. The stages should feel like one continuous illustrated workflow rather than six separate panels.

Suggested arrangement:

**1 → 2 → 3** on one side, flowing across to **4 → 5 → 6** on the other side.

Use curved red arrows and organic connectors to guide the eye.

Avoid:

* six rigid rectangular panels
* heavy borders
* corporate flowchart aesthetics
* excessive whitespace
* an extremely tall composition
* excessive technical or decorative detail

---

## Visual style

Use a **simplified, slightly hand-drawn scientific illustration style**.

The style should be less high-fidelity and less detailed than a polished editorial illustration. Aim for:

* clean, organic linework
* simplified technical forms
* restrained sketch/ink character
* minimal shading
* muted colours
* strong visual hierarchy
* scientifically credible but deliberately simplified objects

The result should look like a **professional scientific poster illustration**, not photorealistic artwork or a generic vector infographic.

Simplify secondary details aggressively. Only include details that communicate the workflow or are scientifically important.

### Colour palette

* arrows

# Six stages

## 1. Packing

Show a museum worker placing a **fossil skull**, such as a dinosaur or wolf skull, into a cardboard container.

A small QR code / identification label should appear on the outside of the box, but it is only a contextual detail and should not attract attention.

The scene should simply communicate:

**museum artefact → cardboard container**

Title: **Packing**

---

## 2. Transport

Show cardboard containers being transported on a pallet, with a simple **van** conveying them to the X-ray scanning facility.

Keep this scene simple and schematic.

The important concept is:

**packed artefact → transport → scanning facility**

Title: **Transport**

Do not add explanatory labels such as “Multiple containers on pallet.”

---

## 3. Loading

Show the containers at the scanning facility and an industrial robotic arm transferring a container toward the X-ray scanner.

### Critical technical detail

The robot must be **suspended from an overhead/ceiling-mounted gantry**.

It must **not** be a conventional floor-mounted industrial robot.

The box should be shown being **lifted vertically by the overhead robot**.

The overhead gantry should be clearly visible so the robotic architecture is unambiguous.

The scene should communicate:

**pallet → overhead robot → lifted box → scanner**

Title: **Loading**

Avoid unnecessary technical labels. The visual itself should communicate the mechanism.

---

## 4. Scanning

Show the cardboard container positioned in an industrial/scientific **X-ray scanner**.

Clearly depict the basic:

**X-ray source → object → detector**

geometry.

The scanner should resemble an industrial/scientific CT system, not a medical hospital CT scanner.

Show the skull inside the container.

Include a **small X-ray projection inset** showing a scientifically plausible projection of the skull. A subtle indication of multiple projections from different angles is acceptable.

Title: **Scanning**

Do not label the scanning stage as rotating.

---

## 5. Reconstruction

Show X-ray projection images flowing into a computational reconstruction process and producing a **voxelised 3D volume of the skull**.

The voxel volume should visibly communicate discrete volumetric data using a subtle grid/voxel structure or partially transparent representation.

The conceptual sequence should be immediately clear:

**X-ray projections → reconstruction → voxelised 3D volume**

Use a simple scientific computer/server representation, but do not over-detail it.

Title: **Reconstruction**

Do not use the phrase or label “computational cluster.”

---

## 6. Compression and Storage

Show the reconstructed voxel volume branching into two data paths:

**Compression**

and

**No compression / uncompressed**

The two branches then converge on a common **long-term archival storage system**, such as servers, storage infrastructure, or an archive/database symbol.

The key visual concept is:

**voxel volume → two data paths → long-term archive**

The distinction between the two paths should be visually clear but understated.

Title:

**Compression and Storage**

Do **not** add explanatory text describing the datasets as “smaller,” “full-resolution,” or similar. Let the visual branching communicate the distinction.
